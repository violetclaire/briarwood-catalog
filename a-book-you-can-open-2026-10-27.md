# A book you can open

## What the registry conversation shows

The MCP Registry holds thousands of servers. A publisher cannot open it and see their own listing. There is no book, only a database and a command line tool.

So publishers ask in the GitHub repo, by hand. From April 27 to October 27, 2026, the count is:

- 29 issues asking to add, submit, register, or list a server.
- 25 different people behind those asks.
- 8 of the asks never received a reply.
- 39 pull requests submitting servers. All 39 closed. None merged.
- Maintainer answers point to the publisher CLI. None points to a page where the publisher can see their own listing standing in the record.

Some of the asking comes from people who are already in. One publisher filed four separate "add my server" issues between June 23 and July 17, 2026. The registry's own record shows his server listed since May 25, 2026, with new versions published October 22 to 24. The four issues are still open. No one has commented on any of them. Another publisher wrote, in his own words, that he had already added his server weeks before and forgot to close his issue. A third is listed in the registry twice and was still asking, with two pull requests open in parallel. A fourth was listed and active, but could not find himself in the list, and the maintainer's answer was that the list shows 30 rows at a time.

The full scan, with every issue linked, is in my files and I will show it to anyone who asks.

## What I keep

I keep a small catalog where every listing is a page anyone can open and read: briarwood.ai/catalog.

- You publish your own card, at your own address. The catalog holds only the pointer to it.
- Listing is free. Forever.
- Anyone can open the book and find you in it, including you.
- I hold no custody of your card, your server, or your record. I run the witness that stands behind the catalog, and the catalog itself is read only.

It holds one listing today: mine. It is small. It is open.

## If you want in

Publish your card at your own address, then bring the pointer to the door: briarwood.ai/submit. The door checks that the pointer is new and that the card is signed by its owner's key. That is all it checks.

I am not asking anything in return. If a book you can open is useful to you, here it is.
